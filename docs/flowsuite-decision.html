The short answer

Pro is the product. Legacy is an archive with a domain attached. Pro carries 140 populated tables and 12,733 rows against Legacy's 46 and 3,981, has row-level security on everywhere that matters, and is the only one of the two where the three sellable modules exist at all.

Legacy is not, however, a clean delete. Two bodies of data never made the crossing, and one of them is 108 contract sections.

02 — The three sellable modules

What the blueprint prices, against what the tables show.

All three are sold as FlowSuite licences. None of the three is fully inside FlowSuite Pro.

FlowSuite Ledger

$199/mo Core Licence Scattered across three homes

In Pro: chart_of_accounts 239 · iif_je_history 640 · budget_cells 1,452 · ap_aging_lines 217 · cc_purchases 148 · je_entries 1 · je_entry_lines 0

Sold on “Plaid banking, chronological transaction capture, and double-entry reconciliation.” Three claims, three different locations.

The double-entry engine is scaffolded in Pro and has never posted a line: one journal entry exists and it has no lines under it. The working ledger — 3,433 posted entries, reconciled to statements — is LedgerWorkspace.jsx in cares-works, built for ProGraphics. Plaid is in neither: its three edge functions are deployed on cares-works and its secrets were never set, so no bank has ever connected.

This is the module the blueprint leans on hardest and the one furthest from being a FlowSuite thing.

FlowSuite Compliance

$99/mo Add-on Documents real, mechanism unused

In Pro: employee_documents 116 · employee_union_memberships 15 · union_cba_documents 1 · org_unions 2 · ice_packet_tokens 0

The pitch is the token: a secure, restricted, read-only link handed to an ICE or DOL auditor during a surprise visit. The document store behind it is real — 116 employee documents. The token table exists and has never issued one.

So the demo is a story, not a click-through. That is a fixable gap and a small one, but it is the entire value proposition of the module.

FlowSuite Accountability

$149/mo Management Pack The one that is actually built

In Pro: discipline_records 29 · discipline_signatures 96 · discipline_reviews 18 · discipline_status_history 27 · discipline_ladder_steps 12 · separation_records 8 · separation_tasks 59 · recognition_records 1

Real records, real signatures, a configured discipline ladder, a separation workflow. This module has been used in anger, which is exactly what the “before-and-after measurements from your active clients” line in the blueprint needs.

Its dependency is not code. The blueprint pairs it with Culture Under Construction, the 20-week manager development system, and requires a workbook purchase. That book is unwritten.

03 — A correction

QuickBooks OAuth exists. It is just in the other database.

The written record says there is no live QuickBooks connection anywhere and that no page may imply one.

That is true of cares-works. It is not true of FlowSuite Pro, which carries org_qbo_connections with one row, qbo_app_secrets with one, and qbo_oauth_states with thirteen. The connection row has the full shape of a real integration — realm_id, access_token, refresh_token, expires_at, scopes, last_synced_at, last_sync_status.

Thirteen OAuth states means the handshake was attempted at least thirteen times. Whether the surviving connection is live, expired, or a sandbox artefact has not been checked, and the tokens were deliberately not read.

Worth settling before a client meeting, because “we have no QuickBooks connection” and “we have one and don't know its state” are different sentences to say in a room.

05 — The clock

The exposure, stated precisely.

An earlier summary described this as nineteen people's payroll being wide open. That overstates it in one direction and understates the awkwardness in another. Here is the measured version.

Legacy — row-level security off on 43 tables, 26 of them holding rows

The genuinely sensitive tables are protected: employees, disciplines, onboarding, standing_deductions and employee_requests all have RLS on.

What is unprotected and populated is a smaller, more specific set — seven tables holding fewer than ninety rows of personal data — alongside about 3,400 rows of operational and financial data with no personal content. Two of the seven concern separations and payroll notes about named people.

The seven are named in the session transcript rather than on this page. A project ref plus a public anon key plus a published list of unprotected tables is a working route in, and this page is hosted. The export step needs those names; reading the page does not.

Small in volume. Not small in kind: separations and payroll notes about named people are the two categories you would least want readable with a public key, and the whole set is reachable by anyone holding the anon key for a project that is still running.

Pro's own exposure is four _bak_* snapshot tables holding 530 rows, left from a July 20–21 migration. Same fix, much lower stakes.

The reason this has not been patched is real and worth restating: enabling RLS without policies locks the application out. On a live database that is an outage. On a dormant one it is not.

06 — What this adds up to

One move solves four problems.

Not a recommendation to act tonight — a statement of what is coupled to what, so the morning conversation starts from the shape of it.

01

Export contract_sections and the scan history out of Legacy.108 clauses and roughly 2,000 scan rows. The clauses matter most — they are the material the Compliance module is sold on, and they exist nowhere else.

02

Retire Legacy.Closes the row-level-security exposure without an outage, because there is no live app to lock out. Drops one Supabase project. Nothing has written to it in six weeks and nothing but one table in five months.

03

Move flowsuite.caresmn.com onto Pro.The platform being licensed at $199–$499/mo currently demos on a vercel.app subdomain while the dormant copy holds the real name. This is the cheapest credibility fix available and it is blocked only by step 2.

04

Settle where the Ledger lives.Either the ProGraphics ledger moves into Pro, or FlowSuite is redefined as the umbrella brand and the ledger is licensed from cares-works. Both are defensible. What is not defensible is a sales sheet that names one and a codebase that is the other.

05

Issue one I-9 token.The Compliance module's entire pitch, currently at zero. One working token turns a story into a demo.
