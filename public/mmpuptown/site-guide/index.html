How to update the website

You can change any wording and any photo on the site yourself, from a browser. Nothing to install, and nobody to wait for.

Your link

The link in the panel is what turns editing on. Bookmark it.

  • Edit mode follows you. Once you’re in it, clicking to another page keeps you in it.
  • Drop the ?edit=jessie part and you see the site exactly as a customer does.

Treat the link like a shared password. There’s no username or sign-in yet, so anyone who has it can change the site. Don’t post it anywhere public.

Changing the words

In edit mode, every bit of text you can change shows a dashed outline.

  1. Click the text. A cursor appears and you type, like in a Word document.
  2. Click Save & Publish in the bar at the bottom.

It’s live the moment you save. No second step, no approval queue. Made a mistake? Click back in and fix it the same way.

Where this works

Nine pages: Home, About, Mission, Products, Print Store, News, Contact, Request a Quote, and the union shop. Around 417 pieces of text and photo.

Where it doesn’t

The menu across the top, the footer, and the three Shop pages (product grid, product pages, cart). Those are built from code, so there’s nothing to click.

Changing a photo

Photos work the same way, with an orange dashed outline instead.

  1. Click the photo, or the empty box where one should go.
  2. Pick a file from your computer. JPG or PNG.
  3. It uploads and appears straight away. Then Save & Publish.

The logo at the top of every page is swapped the same way.

On size: photos are used at full width, so something around 1200 pixels wide looks sharp. A small photo pulled off a phone screenshot will look soft. Photos straight from a phone camera are plenty big.

Checking your work, and the yellow notes

The edit bar has a Preview toggle. It hides the editing marks so you see the page the way a customer does. Reloading without the ?edit=jessie part does the same.

Yellow notes

Headed “JESSIE:” — suggestions about what a section still needs.

Orange tags

Like “image needed” — a spot still waiting on something.

Customers never see either one. They only appear in edit mode. Leave them as long as they’re useful, and delete them once a section is done.

A few notes mention “Christine’s sitemap.” Christine planned the original page layout; those references were left in because they explain why a page is built the way it is.

The Ask button in the corner

Bottom right of every page is a small Ask button. A visitor clicks it, types a question, and gets an answer straight away instead of hunting through the site or waiting for the shop to open.

The version on the site is a cut-down one: it asks the visitor for fewer details, so they can ask a quick question without filling anything in.

The full version collects more from whoever asks — their email so the conversation can be followed up, file attachments, a saved PDF of the conversation, and SMS alerts. A question at ten at night becomes a lead instead of a visitor who leaves. See it at chat.karikounkel.com.

If this stops working

Don’t get stuck, and don’t worry about breaking anything. This editor is new, and now and then it will misbehave.

If edit mode won’t turn on, if Save & Publish doesn’t stick, or if a page won’t let you change something:

  1. Stop retrying. Nothing you do here can damage the site.
  2. Write down what you want changed — which page, what it says now, and what it should say instead.
  3. Send it over and it gets done.

Any format is fine. A Word document, a Google Doc, an email, a list in a text message, even a photo of a printout with your marks on it. Screenshots help — enough of the page to see which bit you mean.

Same goes for anything the editor can’t reach: a new page, a menu change, store products or prices, or notes you want added for whoever edits next.

Never hold a change back because the tool is being awkward. Getting it in writing is always good enough.
