Let's be honest first

I'm not going to pretend I already know your business.

Most proposals you'll ever get open by telling you what's wrong with you. That's a neat trick, considering the person writing it has never seen your bank feed, your filing situation, or the drawer where the mail goes to think about its life.

I'd rather tell you the truth: I don't know where you're at yet. I know you're carrying more administrative weight than you want to carry, and I know you didn't start this business because you love filing. Everything past that is a guess — and guesses are expensive when they're priced by the month.

So here's how I work. We look first, together, in one honest sitting. Then I write down what I actually found. Then, and only then, do we talk about what ongoing help looks like — and what it costs. You'll know exactly what you're buying, because we'll both have seen it.

— Kari Hoglund Kounkel, CARES Consulting Inc

Step one · The Look

One sitting. Thirteen questions. A real answer.

Below is the read I use to find out where a business actually stands. It takes about fifteen minutes. Answer straight — nobody's grading you, and "sorta" is a legitimate answer that tells me a great deal. When you're done, scroll to the bottom of it and it will tell you what it sees.

Band A

Is it getting done at all?

The floor. Whether the necessary paperwork of running a business is happening — by anyone, in any fashion.

1
Mail, paperwork, and documents have a home — and that home is not a pile.
2
Bills get paid on time, without anyone having to go hunting for them first.
3
Invoices go out on a schedule — not whenever somebody happens to remember.
4
Payroll and contractor payments run without a scramble.
5
I could tell you right now, without looking, roughly what's in the account.
Band B

Is anybody checking it?

The middle. Whether the work that's getting done is also getting verified — and whether it would survive you being unavailable.

6
The books get reconciled every month, and a human being signs off on it.
7
I know who owes me money and how long they've owed it.
8
Contracts, licenses, insurance, and renewals are tracked somewhere other than my memory.
9
If I disappeared for two weeks, somebody else could find everything and keep it moving.
Band C

Is anybody steering?

The top. Whether the numbers are being used to make decisions — or just recorded after the decisions get made.

10
I know which parts of this business actually make money, and which ones only look like they do.
11
I have a budget I check against — not just a bank balance I react to.
12
I can see thirteen weeks out on cash, not just today.
13
Big calls — hiring, pricing, expanding, taking on debt — get run through the numbers before they get made.
Your read

Answer the thirteen above and your read appears here.

Yes counts as a full point, sorta as a half, no as zero. The band that scores lowest is the band we start in — that's the whole method.

Why this matters before we price anything

Admin help and CFO help are not the same animal

People use "I need help with the business side" to mean four completely different jobs. Here's the honest ladder, bottom to top. Most businesses need one rung more than they think, and one rung less than they fear.

The rungWhat it actually doesYou need it when…
Administrative Mail, filing, data entry, invoicing, chasing paperwork, keeping the calendar and the renewals honest. Things are falling through the cracks because nobody has the hours.
Bookkeeping Coding transactions, reconciling accounts, keeping AR and AP current, closing the month. The entries exist but nobody trusts the reports that come out of them.
Controller Reviewing the bookkeeper's work, tightening the close, building the process, catching what's wrong before it ships. The work gets done, but nobody is checking it — and you're the backstop by default.
Fractional CFO Cash forecasting, budgets and variance, pricing and margin analysis, profitability by line, lender and board packages, sitting in on the big decisions. The books are fine. You're still flying by feel on the calls that actually matter.
Sidebar note

Hiring a CFO to fix a filing problem is like hiring an architect to find your keys. Both are real professions. Only one of them is going to help you get out the door this morning. The read above exists so we don't make that mistake with your money.

Straight from the top of the ladder

If it turns out you need the CFO seat

You said the ongoing help might be more CFO than not. That may well be right — but it's worth knowing exactly what that word buys, and what it does not. I'd rather draw the line now than have you find it later.

What a fractional CFO does

  • Builds and maintains 13-week and annual cash forecasts
  • Creates and manages the budget — and explains the variances
  • Models the big decisions: pricing, hiring, expansion, debt
  • Analyzes profitability by product, service line, or department
  • Designs the dashboard so you see the few numbers that matter
  • Prepares packages for lenders, investors, or a board
  • Names the financial risks out loud and proposes the fix
  • Translates all of it into plain language you can act on

What it does not do

  • Day-to-day bookkeeping and transaction entry
  • AP and AR data entry
  • Monthly bank reconciliations
  • Payroll processing
  • Business tax return preparation
  • Running routine reports out of your accounting software
  • Chasing overdue invoices and collections follow-up

Which is exactly why we look first. If that right-hand column is where your pain actually lives, a CFO retainer would be an expensive way to stay frustrated. CARES covers both columns — but they get staffed differently, and they get priced differently.

How this runs

Three phases, and you can stop after any of them

🔍
Phase 1

The Look

A fixed-fee, fixed-scope diagnostic. No retainer, no commitment, no meter running.

  • We walk the thirteen questions together and I ask the follow-ups
  • I go through what exists — books, systems, filing, who does what
  • I find what's missing, what's duplicated, and what's quietly costing you
  • You get a written Findings & Plan — what I found, what to fix first, what it takes
  • The document is yours to keep whether or not you hire me for anything else
🧹
Phase 2

The Catch-Up

Only if the Look finds a backlog. Scoped and priced off real findings, not a guess.

  • Clean up whatever's behind — books, filing, AR, unopened obligations
  • Put the systems in that keep it from happening again
  • Write down who does what, so the process outlives everyone's memory
  • A defined project with an end date — not a subscription
🧭
Phase 3

Ongoing Support

The monthly seat. Weighted toward admin, toward CFO, or both — based on what we found.

  • A standing monthly rhythm, so nothing depends on you remembering
  • The admin lane, the CFO lane, or a blend — the read decides the mix
  • A monthly sit-down where the numbers get explained, not just delivered
  • Reviewed at 90 days and adjusted — the mix should change as you get healthier

The honest part: most people in your position want to skip to Phase 3 and just start paying someone. I understand the impulse — it feels like relief. But a monthly retainer set before anybody looked is how businesses end up paying strategist rates for filing work, or filing rates for a problem that needed a strategist. Fifteen minutes of honesty now saves you a year of the wrong help.
